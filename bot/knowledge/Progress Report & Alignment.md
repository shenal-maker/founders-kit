# **Title AI — Progress Report & Alignment** 

Prepared by Adele  |  February 2026

## **Purpose**

This report summarizes everything learned so far from Blueprint Title and Morehead Title, identifies where things stand with each, and surfaces the open questions we need to align on before doing any further outreach.

## **What We've Learned**

### **The Industry (from Steve, CEO of Blueprint Title)**

Steve is a lawyer-turned-title-agent who self-studied title insurance for a year before hiring anyone. He is one of the most knowledgeable people we have spoken to.

#### ***Where the real opportunity is***

* 95% of title AI startups focus on the 6-day title search process. The real work — and the real opportunity — is in the 30-day escrow process.  
* *"Title is dumb. Escrow is hard."*  
* Escrow is where human capital costs are highest and communication overhead is most painful.

#### ***The actual pain point is inbound communication***

* *"Inbound communication is where efficiency goes to die."*  
* Root cause: the average US realtor sells only 5 homes per year, so they don't really know what they're doing, which creates fear, which creates reactive phone calls and emails.  
* Proactive communication — sending updates before clients ask — is the real solution, not just faster responses.

#### ***The most interesting unsolved problem***

* Predictive file management: models that flag which files will fall off the rails before they do, using variables like seller age/tech comfort, property history, credit score, and task timing.  
* *"I am interested in models that can predict if a file's going to fall off the rails... I can build a Henry Ford conveyor belt for a file."*

#### ***What doesn't work as a pitch***

* Fraud prevention — *"everyone says fraud because they're afraid of it, but it's usually not the real problem. If you ask how many fraud cases they've had in a year, the answer is often zero."*  
* Replacing production software entirely — *"no title agency would allow someone to fully replace their production software."*  
* The SF "move fast" approach — *"that approach doesn't work in highly regulated industries like insurance."* Doma/States Title failed because they ignored regulators.

#### ***What Steve recommended we do***

* Talk to 15–20 more people before building anything.  
* Do a minute-by-minute study of escrow officers' time — ask them to estimate task durations, then externally validate.  
* Build very specific features, not a full company, until we have deeper industry credibility.  
* *"I implore you — you're so much smarter than most of the people doing title AI. Work on the harder project."*

#### ***On starting our own title agency***

* Need an LLC and an underwriter willing to vouch for us.  
* Credibility gap is real as undergrads with no industry track record.  
* Possible path: collaboration with an existing title company could circumvent the underwriter requirement.

### **Morehead Title — Relationship Status**

* **Who they are:** NC-based title agency, team of 40–70 year olds, no tech innovation, still entirely on SoftPro. Medium-sized, transaction volume fluctuates seasonally.  
* **Our contacts:** A lawyer (father of Adele's professor) and a title agent. Both Thomas and Adele have been on the email chains.

#### ***What we learned from them***

* Primary pain: manually typing everything into SoftPro, starting with PTO → commitment conversion.  
* During busy season: staying late, overwhelmed by email volume — both inbound client/realtor communication and coordination with attorneys and lenders.  
* Qualia pitched to them and they were interested, but couldn't adopt it due to banking/accounting incompatibility with SoftPro.  
* SoftPro API is private and expensive. Screen-scraping or UI automation would be the alternative path for any SoftPro integration.

#### ***Why calls fell off***

* Mutual — we didn't know what to build, they couldn't tell us, no clear next step was set. Last call was December 2025\.  
* Current status: relationship has gone cold but is salvageable. They never had a bad experience with us — it just faded.
