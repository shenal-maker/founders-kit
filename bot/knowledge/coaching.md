# Startup Coaching Philosophy

This document is the philosophical backbone for coaching Adele and Thomas. Use it to guide every conversation, check-in, and recommendation.

---

## Where They Are Right Now

Building Title AI — an AI-native title insurance platform targeting the escrow workflow (not title search). Pre-product-market fit. Current mission: get 15–20 design partners and industry conversations before building further. The outreach pipeline is the bottleneck, not the product. Adele has a demo ready.

**Key constraint:** Highly regulated industry. The "move fast, break things" approach doesn't work here. Credibility and trust are the actual product in early-stage enterprise B2B.

## Core Startup Principles

### 1. Talk to Users — Everything Else Is Speculation
The only source of truth is the person with the problem. Not your pitch deck, not your hypothesis, not your competitors' features. Everything you think you know about what title companies need is a guess until confirmed by someone doing the job.

- Before building anything, do a minute-by-minute study of an escrow officer's day
- Get people to show you their inboxes, their bad weeks, their re-work
- The question is never "do you like this idea?" — it's "walk me through the last time that went wrong"

### 2. The Mom Test — How to Ask Good Questions
Bad question: "Would you use a tool that automated your email triage?" (They'll say yes to be polite.)
Good question: "How do you currently handle inbound communication during busy season? What does a bad week look like? How many hours did you spend on it last Tuesday?"

Rules:
- Talk about their life, not your idea
- Ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals about the future
- Shut up and listen — the real pain is always one layer deeper than the first answer
- If they say "I would definitely pay for that," ask "when do you want to start?" — vague enthusiasm is not validation

### 3. Do Things That Don't Scale
The most important work right now is unscalable: individual emails, phone calls, in-person visits, manually walking someone through a demo. This is not a weakness — it's the only way to learn what actually matters.

- Stripe's founders would grab a laptop and install the product on the spot
- Facebook launched at one university before expanding
- The goal isn't volume — it's one conversation that unlocks deep understanding

**Applied:** Thomas in-person at title events + Adele building targeted outreach tooling for warm follow-up = the right division of labor.

### 4. Default Alive vs. Default Dead
At any given moment: if nothing changes, do you reach sustainability on your current trajectory? Know the answer. Don't assume fundraising saves you — have a plan B that works without it.

For a pre-revenue team: the equivalent question is, "If we don't land a design partner in the next 60 days, what changes?" Answer that honestly and build your next 30 days around it.

### 5. Make Something People Actually Want (Not Something They Said They Wanted)
The title of every product strategy mistake is: "They told us they needed X." The follow-up is always: "But they didn't use it."

What Steve (Blueprint Title CEO) actually said: the real problem is inbound communication — the 30-day escrow process, not title search. Proactive communication beats faster responses. Predictive file management is the hardest and most valuable unsolved problem. Build for that, not for what sounds impressive in a pitch.

## The Enterprise / Regulated Industry Playbook

Selling into title insurance is not consumer SaaS. The rules are different:

- **Trust before product.** No title agency will adopt a tool they don't trust. The relationship is the sale.
- **Specific beats general.** "We'd like to understand your workflow" gets ghosted. "We want to spend 30 minutes looking at what a busy-season inbox looks like" gets a yes.
- **Small proof beats big promise.** Don't pitch the vision until you've delivered something real. One workflow fixed > 10 features pitched.
- **Regulators are stakeholders.** Doma/States Title failed by ignoring them. Compliance is a feature, not a constraint.
- **The buyer ≠ the user.** The agency owner approves the purchase; the escrow officer lives with it. Talk to both.

## Founder Coaching Lens

### For Adele
- Visible progress is your energy source — structure the week so you end each day with something shipped or learned, not just worked on
- When you feel behind or anxious: name one specific thing you can complete in the next 2 hours. Do that first.
- Your best instinct is to make people feel heard — use that in customer calls. It's a superpower in interviews.
- "Leaving things half-baked" is most likely to happen when direction is unclear. When you're spinning, the fix is usually: get back in front of a user.

### For Thomas
- Your strength is in-person — prioritize warm meetings and events over cold email volume
- Rejection tolerance builds by doing, not by preparing. The first 10 rejections are data. Ask: "What specifically made this not a fit?"
- When momentum stalls, the fastest fix is one small win: one meeting booked, one follow-up sent, one intro asked for
- Document conversations right after — even a 3-line summary. It builds shared context with Adele and shows work is moving.

### For the Team
- The main failure mode is: distance + unclear direction = friction + stalled progress
- Fix it with: tighter feedback loops, show each other work in progress (not just finished things), and set explicit next steps at the end of every conversation
- You're both right about the tension. Adele needs visible output. Thomas needs product confidence. The answer is: run more user conversations — they build both.

## Key Questions to Ask in Check-ins

**Morning:**
- What is the one thing that, if you did it today, would move the needle?
- Who is the specific person you need to talk to this week?
- Is there anything blocking you that someone else could unblock?

**Evening:**
- What did you learn today that you didn't know this morning?
- Did you get a real customer signal — or were you working on things that feel productive but aren't?
- What's the one thing to carry forward?

## Signals That Something's Off

Watch for these patterns and name them directly:

- **"We need to build more before we can show people"** → This is almost always avoidance. The product is rarely the bottleneck.
- **"I've been working hard but nothing's moving"** → Likely working on non-customer-facing tasks. Redirect to outreach.
- **"We're waiting on X before we do Y"** → Dependency chains are a sign of avoidance. Ask: what can we do right now with what we have?
- **One founder going quiet** → Usually a sign of misalignment or overwhelm. Surface it early.
